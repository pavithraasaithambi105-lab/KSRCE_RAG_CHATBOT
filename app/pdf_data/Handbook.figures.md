## Page 9 | Figure 5.1 Process to Define Vision and Mission Statements of the Institute
Diagram type: flowchart.
Inputs (all feed into "Framing of Institute Vision and Mission"):
- Management
- Alumni
- Students
- Parents Feedback
- Local & Global needs
- Faculty
- Academic Peers
- Employer
- Program Outcomes
Steps:
1. Framing of Institute Vision and Mission (using all the inputs above).
2. Formulated and reviewed by VAC (Vision Advisory Committee).
3. Decision "Is it satisfactory?" - If No: go back to step 1, Framing of Institute Vision and Mission. If Yes: go to step 4.
4. Publish & Disseminate Institute Vision and Mission.
Summary: The institute's vision and mission are framed from the views of management, alumni, students, parents, faculty, academic peers, employers, local and global needs and program outcomes; the VAC reviews the draft and it is reworked until satisfactory, then published.

## Page 10 | Figure 6.1 Process to define Program Educational Objectives (PEOs) of the Department
Diagram type: flowchart.
Inputs to "Formulation of Program Educational Objectives":
- Institute & Department Vision and Mission
- Program Outcomes
- Alumni, Students, Parents Feedback
- Faculty, Academic Peers, Employer
Steps:
1. Formulation of Program Educational Objectives (PEOs).
2. Decision "Are Institute & Department Vision & Mission correlated to PEOs?" - If No: go back to step 1, Formulation of Program Educational Objectives. If Yes: go to step 3.
3. Finalized by DAC (Department Advisory Committee) and forwarded to IQAC.
4. Approved by IQAC (Internal Quality Assurance Cell).
5. Publish & Disseminate Program Educational Objectives.
Summary: A department's PEOs are formulated from the institute and department vision and mission, program outcomes, and feedback from alumni, students, parents, faculty, academic peers and employers; they must correlate with the vision and mission, then are finalized by the DAC, approved by IQAC and published.

## Page 71 | Figure 16.1.1 Teaching and Learning Process
Diagram type: process map (seven branches under "TEACHING AND LEARNING PROCESS").
A. Adherence to Academic Calendar (steps in order):
  1. Obtaining University notification through web
  2. Preparation of Academic Calendar by Academic Team
  3. Approval from Principal and HoDs
  4. Circulating the Academic calendar to faculty members and students for preparing semester planner
  5. Course Plan and Course material preparation
B. Instructional Methods and Pedagogical initiatives:
  - Lecturing
  - Assignment, Quiz and Tutorials
  - Tutorials for problematic subjects
  - Mini & Major Projects
  - Simulation tool usage
  - Guest Lectures, Workshops, Value Added Courses
  - Industrial visits, In-plant training and Internships
  - Assigning seminars to students
C. Encouraging Bright Students:
  - Merit scholarships, Cash awards and Prizes
  - Enhancing their extra and co-curricular activities
  - Encouraging to get University ranks
  - Coaching for Competitive exams
C. Supporting Weak Students:
  - Motivation and Mentoring
  - Remedial classes
  - Interaction with Parents
  - Simplified study materials
D. Quality of Class room teaching:
  - Lecture with Discussion
  - Lecture with Demonstration
  - Lecture with videos from NPTEL and internet
  - Activity based teaching
  - Group Discussion
  - Guest Lectures
  - Smart Board Teaching
  - Project Design
E. Conducting Experiments in the laboratory:
  - Verifying observation note book
  - Checking Algorithms and Programs
  - Output Verification with different test cases
  - Explaining the relevance to real life and applications
  - Conducting Viva Voce
F. Continuous Assessments in the Laboratory:
  - Assessing technical performance of the students
  - Conducting Pre-Lab and Post-Lab Viva voce
  - Model Practical Examination
G. Students Feedback and Action taken:
  1. Collecting feedback from the students based on various parameters for 10 points.
  2. Decision "Is the total marks > 6.0?" - If Yes: motivating the faculty to continue the same way of teaching. If No: corrective actions are taken by the HoD and Principal.
Summary: The teaching and learning process covers following the academic calendar, varied instructional methods, encouraging bright students and supporting weak students, classroom teaching quality, laboratory experiments and continuous lab assessment, and student feedback (score out of 10; above 6.0 the faculty continue, otherwise the HoD and Principal take corrective action).

## Page 72 | Academic process: from curriculum to course delivery, assessment and evaluation
Diagram type: process diagram with grouped boxes and a decision.
Flow:
1. Inputs: University Curriculum and Department Academic Calendar.
2. Subject Allocation based on the Specialization.
3. Lesson Plan and Course File Preparation, which includes: Course Outcomes; Mapping of COs & POs; Lesson Plan; Content Beyond Syllabus; Delivery Methods / Models Planned; Guest Lectures, Workshops; Time Table; Course Material; List of Text Books, Reference Books, Journals & Websites; e-materials, Video in DVDs; Scope for Self Learning; Past University Question Papers; Question Bank; Assignment / Work Sheet / Tutorial Questions.
4. Teaching and Learning Modes:
   - Teaching Modes: Black Board Teaching; Audio Visual Aids; Power Point Presentation; Exhibits of model; Correlation with Real Life Examples; Demonstration.
   - Learning Modes: Self learning; Collaborative Learning; Interactive learning; Flipped classes; Group discussions; Technical debate; Students presentation.
5. Verification of Class Room Delivery Effectiveness - Feedback: quality improvement by getting periodic feedback from students.
6. Evaluation Process: Continuous Internal Assessment; End Semester Exam; Practical Exam.
7. Assessment: CO Attainment; PO / PSO Attainment.
8. Decision "Met COs / POs / PSOs?" - If Yes: reset goals to a higher order. If No: identify corrective actions for implementation.
Summary: Subjects are allocated by specialization from the university curriculum and department calendar; faculty prepare the lesson plan and course file, teach using various teaching and learning modes, verify delivery through student feedback, evaluate students through internal, end-semester and practical exams, and measure CO and PO/PSO attainment, either raising goals or taking corrective action.

## Page 78 | Figure 21.10.1 Support Weak Students and Encourage Bright Students
Diagram type: flowchart with two branches.
1. Students are identified based on Academic Performance / Soft Skills / Communication, and split into Weak Students and Bright Students.
2. Weak Students:
   - Mentoring: Moral support and motivation; Interaction with parents.
   - Academic Improvement: Remedial classes; Group studies with bright students and simplified study materials.
   - Skill Development: Basic soft skill development; Value added courses / Workshops / Seminars.
3. Bright Students:
   - Mentoring: Higher studies; Acquire placement; Competitive exams.
   - Academic Improvement: To improve the CGPA with distinction; To attain the university rank.
   - Skill Development: Advanced soft skill training; Value added courses / Workshops / Seminars at reputed colleges.
4. Both branches lead to Collaborative Learning.
5. Result: Increase in the student's performance.
Summary: Students are classified as weak or bright by academic performance, soft skills and communication; each group gets tailored mentoring, academic improvement and skill development, and both join collaborative learning to improve performance.

## Page 92 | Figure 21.15.6 Process for Assessment of Course Outcomes
Diagram type: flowchart.
1. Selection of Course and their Outcomes.
2. Assessment based on levels.
3. Level Classification (Bloom's Taxonomy) into six levels, each with assessment tools:
   - Remembering Level: Internal Assessment; University Examinations; Laboratory Exercise Evaluation.
   - Understanding Level: Internal Assessment; University Examinations; Laboratory Exercise Evaluation; Assignment / Quiz.
   - Applying, Analyzing, Evaluating and Creating Levels: Internal Assessment; University Examinations; Assignments / Quiz / Tutorial / Case Study; Seminar / Mini project / Group Discussion; Laboratory Exercise Evaluation; Project Evaluation; COCAT.
4. Assessing the Attainment Level of CO (Course Outcome).
5. Decision "Whether the calculated attainment level meets the target level?" - If Yes: follow the same teaching methodology. If No: improve the teaching methodology by various instructional methods.
Summary: Course outcomes are assessed at each Bloom's taxonomy level using level-specific tools; CO attainment is calculated and compared with the target, keeping the teaching method if met, or improving it if not.

## Page 130 | Figure 30.4 Process of Conduction and Evaluation of Performance Analysis of Internal Exam
Diagram type: flowchart.
1. Preparation of question paper with COs and Bloom's taxonomy level by the Course coordinator.
2. Quality of question paper is verified by the Module coordinator.
3. Decision "Is the quality of question paper satisfied?" - If No: the Course coordinator makes the corrections on the question paper based on the instructions given by the Module coordinator, and it goes back to step 2. If Yes: go to step 4.
4. Approved by Program Coordinator.
5. Question paper with answer key is forwarded to the exam cell.
6. Conduction of Internal Exams.
7. Evaluation based on answer key.
8. Performance Analysis.
9. Analysis of CO's Attainment.
10. Decision "Are COs attained?" - If Yes: continue the same process. If No: identification of students to improve the COs, then remedial action like improvement test and mentoring are taken.
Summary: Internal exam papers are prepared by the course coordinator, verified by the module coordinator (corrected until satisfactory), approved by the program coordinator, conducted, evaluated with the answer key, and analysed for CO attainment, with remedial actions for students when COs are not attained.

## Page 177 | Fig. 41.6 Department level budget process (institute level budget)
Diagram type: grouped inputs followed by a flowchart.
Inputs from:
- Academic Departments: Laboratory Equipments; Software; R & D; Consumables; Library; Training & Travel; Maintenance & Spares.
- Library: Library Books; Journals (Hard Copy); E-Journals.
- Placement: Training; Expenses related to Campus Interview; Placement Award Function; Travel & Accommodation; Industry Connect, Spark and others if any.
- Purchase Department: Stationery; Awards & Memento; Exam Cell Requirements.
- Physical Education: Sports Goods Requirements; TA / DA.
- Hostel: Vegetables; Provisions; Gas, Wood; EB; Water; Others if any.
- Accounts: Employee Salaries; Advertisement Charges; Infrastructure; Tax; Others if any.
- Transport: Insurance; Permits; Tax; Fuel; FC Charges; Maintenance.
- System Administrator: System Requirements; Maintenance & Spares; Internet; LCD Projectors; Camera / TV; Others if any.
- Maintenance: Plumbing; Electrical; House Keeping; Others if any.
Steps:
1. Submission to the Overall Budget Co-ordinator.
2. Submission to the Principal.
3. Meeting of HODs, In-charges of various activities, Overall Budget Co-ordinator and the Principal.
4. Scrutinization.
5. Finalization of Institute Level Budget.
6. Submission to GC (Governing Council) for approval.
Summary: Budget requirements from all departments and units are submitted to the overall budget co-ordinator and the Principal, discussed in a meeting of HODs and in-charges, scrutinized, finalized as the institute level budget and sent to the Governing Council for approval.

## Page 178 | Fig 41.10 Budget Process - Program Level
Diagram type: grouped inputs followed by a flowchart with a decision loop.
Inputs from:
- Laboratory In-charge: Equipment Requirements; Software; Consumables; Maintenance & Spares.
- Research Co-ordinator: Patent; Student Project; New Facility Requirements.
- Library In-charge: Library Books; Journals.
- Association In-charges: Guest Lecture; Workshop; Symposium; Conference; Seminar; Travel; Visiting Faculty; Club Activities.
- Others if any: Value Added Course; FDP, etc.
Steps:
1. HOD Meeting.
2. Preparation of Department Level Budget Proposal.
3. Submission of Department Budget Proposal to the Overall Budget Co-ordinator.
4. Submission of Department Budget Proposal to the Principal.
5. Meeting with Principal.
6. Decision "Is approved?" - If No: go back to step 2 (prepare the department level budget proposal again). If Yes: submission to the GC (Governing Council) for approval.
Summary: At program level, requirements from lab, research, library and association in-charges are discussed in an HOD meeting, turned into a department budget proposal, submitted to the overall budget co-ordinator and the Principal, revised until approved, and then sent to the Governing Council.
